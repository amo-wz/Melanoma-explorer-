Occult metastatic melanoma: likely source and survival

An explorer built for the case report "Hiding in Plain Bile". It uses 144 patients with metastatic melanoma treated with anti-PD-1 to show how tumour genomics points to a primary site, and how survival changes with the pattern of spread.

Her driver profile fits cutaneous melanoma; a UV signature would confirm it

Each melanoma subtype carries its own drivers and its own share of UV-induced mutations. Her panel found only BRAF V600E. In this cohort, tumours with an occult primary mostly look cutaneous by UV signature.

Methods and limits

  • Data. Clinical file for cBioPortal study mel_dfci_2019: whole-exome sequencing of 144 pre-treatment tumours, patients treated with anti-PD-1 (Liu et al., Nat Med 2019). Downloaded 21 November 2026.
  • Survival. Overall survival from the start of anti-PD-1. Kaplan–Meier estimates with 95% confidence intervals on the log(−log) scale. Medians and their intervals are where the curve and its bands first reach 50%.
  • UV share. UV-induced mutations divided by total mutations per tumour. Occult vs cutaneous: p = 0.64; occult vs acral and mucosal: p < 0.001 (Mann–Whitney).
  • Limits. These are group estimates, not a prediction for one patient. Brain metastases here were present at the start of treatment; hers developed on pembrolizumab. The cohort comes from Western tertiary centres with full access to treatment. Subgroups under 10 patients have very wide intervals.
